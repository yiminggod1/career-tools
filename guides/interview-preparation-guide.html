A Practical Interview Preparation Guide

A structured approach to researching the role, preparing stories, practicing answers and choosing questions.

Interview preparation works best when it produces evidence you can explain naturally rather than memorized scripts. The objective is to know your experience well enough to answer different questions with specific examples, clear reasoning and measurable results.

Research the role first

Review the job description and identify the capabilities the employer is likely to test. Then research the company, team, product or service and the context of the role. Keep notes focused on information that can change how you answer questions.

Build a story library

Prepare several examples covering success, failure, conflict, leadership, problem solving, learning and ambiguity. A small library of strong stories can answer many behavioral questions. For each story, remember the situation, your responsibility, the actions you personally took and the result.

Use STAR without sounding robotic

The STAR structure—Situation, Task, Action and Result—is useful because it keeps answers focused. Spend enough time explaining your own actions, not just what the team did. When possible, quantify the outcome or explain what changed.

Practice difficult questions

Practice aloud rather than only reading model answers. Notice whether you answer the question directly, whether your example is specific and whether you spend too much time on background. The Interview Answer Practice tool can provide a structured review, while the Interview Question Generator can create role-specific prompts.

Prepare questions of your own

Good questions help you understand the role. Ask about priorities, success measures, team structure, current challenges, onboarding and how decisions are made. Avoid asking only questions whose answers are already obvious from the job posting.

Do a final rehearsal

  • Prepare a short introduction.
  • Review five to eight flexible experience stories.
  • Practice the most likely role-specific questions.
  • Prepare several questions for the interviewer.
  • Confirm interview time, format and technology.
  • Review the resume version submitted for the role.
