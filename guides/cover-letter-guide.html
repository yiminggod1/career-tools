How to Write a Better Cover Letter

A practical guide to relevance, evidence, personalization, structure and final review.

A cover letter is most useful when it adds context that the resume cannot provide. It should not repeat every bullet from the resume. Instead, use a short structure that connects the employer's needs with a few pieces of evidence from your experience.

Open with relevance

Start by naming the role and giving one reason your background is relevant. Avoid generic openings that could be sent to any company. The first paragraph should quickly answer why you are contacting this employer for this position.

Choose two or three strong examples

Use the middle of the letter to connect important job requirements with evidence. If the posting emphasizes process improvement, explain a process you improved and the result. If it emphasizes customer relationships, describe the scale or outcome of relevant work.

Personalize without forcing it

Personalization is more than inserting a company name. Mention a product, mission, team priority or role responsibility that genuinely connects with your experience. Do not make claims about a company that you have not verified.

Keep the letter easy to scan

Short paragraphs and concrete examples usually work better than dense blocks of text. Remove repeated phrases and generic adjectives. Every sentence should either establish relevance, provide evidence or make the next step clear.

Review before sending

  • Correct company and role name.
  • Specific evidence rather than generic enthusiasm.
  • No unsupported claims.
  • Consistent tone with the resume.
  • Clear contact information.
  • Proofread names, dates and formatting.
