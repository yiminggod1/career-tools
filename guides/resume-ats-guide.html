How to Make a Resume More ATS-Friendly

A practical guide to resume structure, keywords, readability and human review.

A resume is often reviewed quickly, and applicant tracking systems can add another layer of filtering before a recruiter reads it. That does not mean a resume should be written for a machine alone. The useful goal is a document that is easy for software to parse and easy for a person to understand.

Start with a simple structure

Use recognizable section headings such as Summary, Experience, Education and Skills. Keep dates, job titles and employer names visually consistent. Avoid unnecessary columns, decorative elements, images that contain important text, and unusual symbols when they do not add information. A clean hierarchy helps both automated parsing and human scanning.

Use the language of the target role carefully

Read the job description and identify repeated technical skills, tools, certifications, responsibilities and role-specific terminology. Compare those terms with your real experience. Add relevant terms when they accurately describe work you have actually done; do not insert keywords simply to increase a count.

Turn responsibilities into evidence

Strong bullets normally explain an action and its result. Instead of only saying that you managed a process, explain what changed: volume handled, time saved, error reduction, revenue supported, customers served, projects delivered or another measurable outcome. Numbers are useful when they are accurate and meaningful.

Check readability after keyword optimization

A keyword-rich resume can still be difficult to read. Read the document as a recruiter would: Can the role, level and strongest qualifications be understood in seconds? Are the most relevant achievements near the top? Are bullets concise enough to scan? A practical review should consider both matching and communication.

Use Career Tools for a second pass

You can use the Resume Score, ATS Resume Checker, Resume Keyword Checker and Resume vs Job Matcher to identify different issues. Treat their output as a checklist rather than a hiring prediction. Review every suggestion against the actual job and your own experience before making changes.

Final checklist

  • Section headings are clear and consistent.
  • Contact information is easy to find.
  • Important skills match the target role where truthful.
  • Experience bullets emphasize outcomes, not only duties.
  • Formatting is consistent from top to bottom.
  • The final version is proofread before submission.
