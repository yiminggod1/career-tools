How to Compare Job Offers Beyond Base Salary

A practical framework for comparing salary, bonus, benefits, commute and time costs.

Comparing compensation is more useful when you look beyond the headline salary. Two offers with similar base pay can have very different total value once bonus structure, benefits, commute, time and other costs are considered.

Start with guaranteed compensation

Base salary is usually the easiest number to compare. Record the annual amount and the pay schedule. Then separate guaranteed compensation from variable compensation such as bonuses, commissions or equity. A target bonus should not automatically be treated as guaranteed cash.

Value benefits carefully

Health insurance contributions, retirement matches, paid time off and other benefits can have real financial value. Use a reasonable annual estimate rather than assuming every benefit equals cash. If you cannot reliably assign a value, list it separately instead of inventing precision.

Include work-related costs

Commute expenses are an obvious example. Fuel, transit, parking and vehicle wear can reduce the practical value of a higher salary. Time matters too. An additional hour of commuting each workday can represent hundreds of hours per year.

Consider the compensation structure

Ask when bonuses are paid, what conditions apply, how benefits are calculated and whether variable compensation has a history of being paid. For equity, vesting schedules and grant details matter. These questions are especially important when comparing offers with different compensation mixes.

Use calculators as transparent estimates

The Salary to Hourly Calculator, Total Compensation Calculator, Job Offer Comparison and Remote vs Office Calculator can help put numbers into a common framework. They are estimates, not financial advice, and their results depend on the assumptions you enter.

Make a comparison table

  • Base salary
  • Expected bonus or commission
  • Benefits value
  • Retirement contribution or match
  • Paid time off
  • Commute cost and time
  • Schedule and location
  • Growth, role scope and other non-cash factors
